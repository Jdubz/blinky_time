    Settings

    remembered in this browser

    Label clicks

    Kick clicks. A low click at every kick label during playback. If the click doesn't land on the kick drum, the label is off. Off by default. Shortcut Shift+K.
    Snare clicks. A high click at every snare label. Off by default. Shortcut Shift+S.
    Volumes. The click level and the music level.

    Playback

    Speed. Slow the music down to hear timing. The clicks stay exactly on their labels; the pitch drops. Shortcuts 1 2 3.
    Loop. Repeat the selected region, or what's on screen if nothing is selected. Shortcut L.
    Follow. Scroll the view along with playback.
    Band solo. Play only the kick or snare frequency band, to hear one drum under its clicks. Shortcut B cycles.

    Reviewing

    Review order. What Reviewed → next (Enter) goes to after marking the screen reviewed. "in order": the next screen. "suspect first": the next suspect bar (red or amber in the suspect strip) that isn't reviewed yet; when none are left it goes on in order and says so. Next suspect (J) jumps there either way.
    Expected-hit markers. Hollow markers in the kick and snare lanes where the track's repeating pattern says a hit should be, the audio has an attack, and there's no label. One click on a marker adds it as a label. They're hints: they're never saved. They hide once there's a label within ±30 ms, and in reviewed parts.

    Editing

    Snap on add. A hit you add moves to the nearest strong attack within ±25 ms. N snaps selected hits.
    Snap target. What snapping aligns to. "attack" is the transient both drums share; a kick's low-frequency energy builds up a few ms after it.

    Display

    Reference ticks. Faint ghost ticks at the bottom of each lane from another label set, for comparison. They're not editable.
    Hi-hat ticks. Faint read-only marks along the top of the snare lane where the best automatic labels heard hi-hats. Not labels you're reviewing. Off by default.

    How this page works

    You're checking the kick and snare labels of one track at a time. Each track starts with the best automatic labels available (from a drum transcriber such as ADTOF). Fix what's wrong, mark each screen reviewed, and save. The scorer uses your labels once a track is 100% reviewed and saved.

    Reviewing a track

    1. Pick a track with Tracks (or T). Tracks with the most suspect bars left to check are listed first.
    2. Start with the suspect bars. The thin strip above reviewed marks the bars where the automatic labels are most likely wrong: red = check first, amber = check next. Hover it to see why (for example "2 snares missing where the pattern expects them"). Next suspect (J) jumps to the next one nobody has reviewed; reviewed ones fade. To have Reviewed → next jump there too, set ⚙ Settings → Review order to "suspect first".
    3. Play (Space). To hear where the labels are, turn on label clicks in ⚙ Settings: a low click at each kick label, a high click at each snare label. A click that doesn't sit on its drum is a bad label. Slowing down (2, 3) makes small offsets easier to hear.
    4. Fix what's wrong:
      • add: double-click in the kick or snare lane, or press K / S at the mouse;
      • add an expected hit: hollow markers show where the track's pattern expects a kick or snare that has no label. Listen; if it's really there, click the marker and it becomes a label (or select a region and press A to add every expected hit in it). Markers you leave alone are never saved;
      • move: hover a marker until the pointer turns into a hand and the marker lights up, then drag. In the kick lane you grab kicks, in the snare lane snares, in the waveform either. Dragging empty space selects a region instead;
      • delete: select it and press X;
      • swap kick ↔ snare: select it and press C;
      • nudge: ← / → by 1 ms.
    5. Mark the screen reviewed with Reviewed → next (Enter). It moves on one screen. Green in the bottom lane and the overview shows what you've covered; U jumps to anything skipped.
    6. Write down patterns you notice in Notes (under the lanes), for example "snares under the filter sweep all dropped". Add note attaches it to the selected region, or to the whole track. Notes show as violet marks on the time ruler and the overview; click one in the list to jump there. Everyone sees them and anyone can edit or delete them; they're published with the labels and help tune the automatic labels.
    7. That's it: there's no Save. Every change saves by itself, and everyone works on the same labels: other people's changes show up within a few seconds. The labels are published to the corpus every few minutes; the scorer uses a track once it's 100% reviewed.

    What you're looking at

    overviewThe whole track. Green is reviewed, red and amber are suspect bars, violet is notes; the box is the current view. Click to jump.
    time rulerSeconds. Violet bars under it are notes: hover one to read it.
    spectrogramFrequency over time, 30 Hz–12 kHz. Drums show as vertical streaks.
    waveformThe full mix. Every label is drawn through it as a line.
    kick laneThe 30–150 Hz band: the fill is its loudness, the line its onset strength. Peaks in the line are likely kicks. Kick labels are the markers.
    snare laneThe 150 Hz–5 kHz band, drawn the same way, with the snare labels.
    expected hitsHollow markers in the kick or snare lane: the pattern expects a hit there and the audio has an attack, but there's no label. Click to add. Only a hint.
    suspectBars to check first: red high priority, amber medium; faded once reviewed. Hover for the reason. The overview shows the same colours.
    reviewedGreen where you've marked reviewed, red where you haven't.

    The panels under the lanes show the selection, an offset check (labels consistently early or late against the attack), and checks (two same-drum hits closer than 20 ms; I jumps to the next).
